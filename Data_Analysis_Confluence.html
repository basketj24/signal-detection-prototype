3. The RELAY Blind Spot

71.2% of all claims come from RELAY with no pharmacy name, city, ZIP, NPI, prescriber name, or Rx number. This is the single largest constraint on signal detection capabilities.

RELAY claims DO provide:

  • Full financial data (out-of-pocket, benefit paid, remaining OOP)
  • State-level geography (PHARMA_ST)
  • Date of fill & processing date
  • Drug/NDC information
  • Quantity dispensed

RELAY claims DO NOT provide:

  • Pharmacy identity (name, city, ZIP, NPI)
  • Prescriber identity (name, ID, state)
  • Rx number, member number, group number
  • New/refill indicator
  • Patient status

Implication

The prototype's pharmacy-level views (Top 5 Pharmacies, Pharmacy page, Watch List) can only surface insights from ~29% of claims. The remaining 71% can only be analyzed at the state + cost + volume level — not tied to individual pharmacy actors.

5. Data Quality Concerns

5.1 Negative Financial Values

FieldNegative RecordsLikely Explanation
INITIAL_OUT_OF_POCKET127,763 (12.2%)Claim reversals / adjustments
BENEFIT_PAID127,746 (12.2%)Reversed benefits
REMAINING_OUT_OF_POCKET47,478 (4.5%)Overpayment corrections

These need to be handled in cost-per-claim calculations — either excluded, netted, or flagged separately — to avoid skewing averages.

5.2 Pharmacy Identity Fragmentation

  • 30,613 unique pharmacy names vs. 12,258 unique NPIs
  • Name-based matching will contain duplicates (spelling variations, DBAs, chain vs. individual store names)
  • Without NPI on most records, cross-vendor pharmacy deduplication is unreliable

5.3 Brand Name Inconsistency

7 different DRUG_NM values map to 2 brands:

  • Nurtec: "NURTEC TBDP 000075.000", "NURTEC ODT 75 MG TABLET", "NURTEC TAB 75MG ODT", "Nurtec", "NURTEC Tablet Dis 75MG"
  • Eucrisa: "EUCRISA", "EUCRISA 2% OINTMENT"

Normalization logic is needed to map these to canonical brand names.

7.2 Prototype Design Adjustments

#RecommendationRationale
1Add a "Data Coverage" indicator on pharmacy views (e.g., "Based on 29% of claims with pharmacy-level data")Sets proper expectations for users on what the numbers represent
2Include vendor-level signal detection as a first-class view (not just pharmacy)RELAY claims can still surface cost/volume anomalies at vendor level even without pharmacy IDs
3Separate CO-PAY and VOUCHER signal models — different data = different detection logicVoucher claims need cost/volume heuristics; CO-PAY claims support pharmacy-level profiling

7.3 Future Enhancements (Pending Data Improvements)

  • Pharmacy deduplication engine — once NPI coverage improves, build fuzzy matching + NPI-based linkage to consolidate pharmacy identities across vendors
  • Refill velocity tracking — requires RX_NBR and MEMBER_NBR; only feasible if RELAY provides these or if scoped to CO-PAY only
  • Patient journey mapping — requires PATIENT_STS and member-level linkage; currently only 13% coverage
  • Promotion ROI by signal category — requires PROMOTION field; currently 85% missing

8. Summary

Bottom line: The data strongly supports cost-based and volume-based signal detection across all claims. However, pharmacy-level detection — which is the core of the current prototype — is limited to ~29% of claims due to RELAY's data gaps. Closing the RELAY blind spot is the highest-impact data action for this initiative.

The prototype should be positioned as demonstrating the target-state workflow for signal detection, with the understanding that full pharmacy-level coverage requires upstream data improvements from RELAY.
